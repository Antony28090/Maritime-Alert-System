Suggested text:

> The buffer predicate the dashboard ships is unusable on near-miss trips: at every horizon it raises Danger on three trips in four or more, because the approach leg points at the line and a rollout of even five ticks (≈ 6.7 km at the generator's 1.34 km/tick) reaches the 2 km buffer from a loiter point 3–4 km out. Layer 1 already colours that region CAUTION; a projection into it adds nothing but an alarm.
>
> Restricting Layer 2 to projected *crossings* changes the picture. At H = 5 the false-alert rate is 8–10% for a mean advance of 4.5–5.0 ticks — roughly three times the reactive geofence's 1.7 — and eq. (1) is met exactly by the linear policies. Between H = 5 and H = 10 the false-alert rate jumps to 82–90% and stays there. Fig. 6 plots the two as a trade-off: under the crossing predicate every policy's curve has a knee at H = 5, and beyond it warning is bought only at near-certain false alarm. The design rule of Section VII-C therefore resolves, on this generator, to H ≈ 5 with a crossing-only predicate. The LSTM sits slightly left of the linear policies at every point (fewer false alerts, less warning) — the same contraction that costs it AWT buys it a lower false-alert rate.
>
> Two cautions. The near-miss set is deliberately hard (closest approach 2.05–4.21 km, mean 2.91 km; 13 of 50 trips inside 2.5 km); trips that stayed 5 km out would false-alert far less. And the tick scale is the generator's: at 30-second GPS cadence a 5-tick horizon is 2.5 minutes, at the generator's implied 7–11 minutes per tick it is roughly half an hour. Both are stated in ticks in the tables for that reason.

## 13. Section VIII — Threats to validity

- **VIII-A "No false-alert rate was measured"** → delete; replace with a short "Near-miss set is synthetic and deliberately close" paragraph (closest-approach numbers above; results are for a hard case).
- **VIII-D "In-sample forecast evaluation"** → delete.
- **VIII-D "Single machine, single seed regime"** → keep; note that the LSTM-vs-linear ordering at H = 15 reversed between two retrains on different trip sets, which is the concrete evidence for not trusting one-tick separations — but the ≥ 3-tick LSTM deficit at H ≥ 15 and the H = 5 → 10 false-alert cliff are much larger than that noise.
- Add **"The learned forecaster is the weakest rollout"**: state it as a finding, not a limitation to hide — the 10,702-parameter delta-LSTM is a marginally better one-step predictor and a worse multi-step projector than constant velocity on this generator; the recommended production configuration is therefore a constant-velocity rollout, crossing predicate, H ≈ 5, with Layer 1 unchanged. That is a cheaper system than the one described, and the paper should say so.

## 14. Conclusion

Replace the "two results" paragraph: (i) any rollout policy warns 3–10× earlier than a reactive geofence depending on H; (ii) for linear rollouts AWT = H + AWT_reactive to within a tick, so warning is bought by horizon; (iii) — new — the price is the false-alert rate, which under a crossing-only predicate is ~10% at H = 5 and ≥ 82% at H ≥ 10, fixing the usable horizon at about 5 ticks on this generator; (iv) the learned forecaster under-delivers AWT relative to constant velocity by up to 4.8 ticks, so the paper's recommendation is the cheaper linear rollout. Next steps: logged-GPS pilot; Pi latency measurement; a persistence gate (k consecutive projected crossings) to push the knee to longer H.
